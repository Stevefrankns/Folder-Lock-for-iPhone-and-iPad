Why Mobile File Protection Matters on iOS

An unlocked iPhone is a goldmine of personal data. Even if you use a strong screen passcode, handing your phone to a friend to show a photo, or giving an iPad to a child to play a game, instantly exposes your files, emails, and financial apps.

Apple has historically relied heavily on the primary lock screen (Face ID or Touch ID) to protect the device. While features like the "Hidden Album" in Photos have existed for a while, true, granular folder locking—the ability to put a specific password on a specific folder—has required workarounds.

How to Lock Apps and Folders on iPhone

Depending on whether you are trying to hide a specific photo, lock down your banking app, or create a completely encrypted vault, the method changes.

Difficulty: Easy · Native iOS Feature

Lock Apps with Face ID (iOS 18+)

Starting with iOS 18, Apple allows users to require Face ID, Touch ID, or a passcode to open any app. You can also hide the app entirely, moving it to a hidden, locked folder in the App Library.

  1. Long-press the app icon on your Home Screen.
  2. Tap Require Face ID.
  3. Confirm your biometric scan.
  4. (Optional) Choose Hide and Require Face ID to remove it from the home screen entirely.

Pros

  • Built directly into iOS
  • Protects banking and financial apps easily

Cons

  • Doesn't allow locking individual sub-folders
  • If someone knows your device passcode, they bypass this lock
Difficulty: Moderate · Maximum Security

Create an Encrypted Vault (Third-Party)

For users who need to lock specific files, documents, or photos separately from the device's main passcode, a dedicated app is required. These apps create a "sandbox" on your iPhone that requires a distinct PIN or master password.

This is crucial if your primary concern is someone who already knows your phone's unlock PIN (like a partner or child).

Difficulty: Easy · Built-in

iOS Notes Lock Folder

While you cannot lock an entire "Folder" inside the Apple Notes app, you can lock individual notes.

  1. Open the Notes app and select a note.
  2. Tap the three dots (More) icon in the top right.
  3. Tap Lock.
  4. Use your iPhone passcode or create a separate Notes password.

Biometric vs PIN App Locking — Pros and Cons

Many users wonder if they should rely on Face ID or a custom PIN for their secure folders.

  • Face ID (Biometric): Extremely convenient. However, it shares the security model with your phone. If iOS fails to recognize your face, it defaults to asking for your device PIN. Therefore, a biometric lock is only as secure as your phone's screen password.
  • Custom PIN (Folder Lock): Less convenient, as you must type it manually. However, it is vastly more secure against physical access. A family member who knows your iPhone unlock code still cannot access a third-party app protected by a unique 8-digit PIN.

App Lock Behavior After Phone Restart

A common vulnerability with some older Android app lockers was that restarting the phone temporarily disabled the lock. iOS sandboxing prevents this. Whether you use native Face ID locking or an app like Folder Lock, restarting your iPhone will force the secure enclave to lock all protected data until the master passcode is entered.

Troubleshooting & Specific iOS Workarounds

We receive many specific queries about locking files across the Apple ecosystem. Here are the genuine solutions to common search console intents.

How to Lock an iPad from Your iPhone

A frequent question is: "Can I lock my iPad from my iPhone?" The answer is yes, but it relies on Apple's Family Sharing and Screen Time features, not a traditional folder lock.

1. On your iPhone, go to Settings > Family.
2. Tap the child/family member's name who uses the iPad.
3. Tap Screen Time.
4. Select "Lock Device" or set immediate "Downtime".
This instantly locks the iPad screen remotely.

To restrict access to specific folders on that iPad locally, you must install a secure folder app directly onto the iPad itself.

Transferring Locked Files: iPhone to iPad

If you have sensitive files stored in Folder Lock on your iPhone and want to send them to an iPad, standard AirDrop will expose the files outside the vault. Instead, use the Wi-Fi Transfer feature built into most secure folder apps:

  1. Open Folder Lock on your iPhone and enable Wi-Fi Transfer.
  2. Open a web browser on your iPad and enter the IP address displayed on the iPhone.
  3. Download the files securely over your local network.

Forgot Your Secure Folder PIN?

If you forget the master PIN for a third-party app like Folder Lock, there is no backdoor to crack the password. This is by design for your security. To recover access, you must have previously set up a recovery email within the app's settings. If you did not, the encrypted data is permanently inaccessible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Folder Lock available for iPhone?

Yes, Folder Lock is available on the iOS App Store. It is specifically designed to create an encrypted, password-protected vault on iPhones and iPads.

Can I create a folder for pictures and lock it on iPhone, apart from the hidden folder?

Not natively. The Apple Photos app only offers the single "Hidden" album. To create multiple distinct, locked folders for different categories of pictures, you must download a third-party app like Folder Lock.

Does Folder Lock for iPhone support Face ID?

Yes. You can configure Folder Lock to open via Face ID for convenience, while retaining a strong alphanumeric password as the master backup.

Can someone bypass app lock by restarting the phone?

No. Due to iOS's strict sandboxing and hardware encryption (Secure Enclave), restarting an iPhone will not bypass native app locks or reputable third-party vaults.

How does Samsung Secure Folder differ from iOS options?

Samsung Secure Folder uses Knox hardware encryption to create a completely separate operating environment (a phone within a phone). iOS does not have an exact native equivalent; relying instead on App Locking (iOS 18) or third-party sandboxed apps to achieve similar results.
